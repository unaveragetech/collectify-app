Scan it. Bind it. Trade it.

Collectify is an Android app for trading-card collectors. Scan cards with your camera, keep them in binders, track what they're worth across 94 TCGs and trade in person. The whole card catalog is bundled, so it works offline.

What it does

One app for the whole hobby loop: find the card, file it, value it, show it off, swap it.

📷

Scanner

Point the camera at a card. On-device OCR reads it, checks the words against the real card names for that game, and tells you how sure it is. If it can't read the card it says so instead of guessing.

📔

Binders

Page-by-page binders with value, search, sleeves, and covers you design with your own cards and stickers.

🔎

Inspect & grade

Zoom into corners, edges and surface, measure centering and record a pseudo-grade. It's your own estimate and is labelled that way.

🤝

In-person trading

Scan a QR on the same Wi-Fi: both phones see the same trade live, either side can agree or leave, and any change resets both agreements. You swap the real cards; the app moves the records.

💲

Prices

Market prices for around half a million cards and products, adjusted for condition. They are estimates from tcgcsv.com (TCGplayer data), not offers.

⛏️

Treasure Box

A game on the side: mine booster packs out of rock, slice the foil, pull cards, tuck them into binders. Game cards are clearly badged, never count toward your collection's value, and trade only as marked DIGITAL cards.

🏝️

Island Care

A small 3D island to look after: grow berries, craft food and toys, and keep your Pokémon fed, watered and happy in a two-minute check-in. Scanning cards feeds the island, but you can play without scanning. No pet ever dies. Your coins are shared with the Treasure Box.

Goals

What we're building toward, and what we won't compromise on.

Your collection stays yours

No accounts and no ads. Your binders, wishlist, grades and trades live on your device and survive updates. The app only goes online to sync prices or fetch card images, and for the update check when you tap it.

Works anywhere

At a card show with no signal, the full catalog, scanner and your binders still work. Everything needed is inside the app.

Honest numbers

Prices are labelled as estimates. A grade you set yourself is a "pseudo-grade". The scanner shows its confidence, and says "couldn't read it" when that's the truth.

Fun that stays in its lane

The Treasure Box is there for engagement, but it is kept visibly separate from your real collection so it never inflates your numbers.

Looking ahead: Collective

Planned A grading service with market data built in: graded cards would be public records, sealed with a tamper-evident seal, with faster turnaround and lower fees than the established graders. Collective is not live: there is no launch date or pricing yet, and until it exists the in-app grades are pseudo-grades only.

How it's built

A few deliberate choices that shape how the app behaves.

Catalog inside the APK

A snapshot of the tcgcsv catalog ships as a SQLite database, so there's nothing to download on first run. Updating the app brings a newer catalog without touching your own data.

Local server + WebView

The app embeds a tiny HTTP server on 127.0.0.1 and shows the same web interface as the desktop version. One codebase for the UI, camera access works because localhost is a secure context.

Scanner = evidence, not a guess

Several OCR passes, fuzzy matching against real card names, where on the card the words were found, collector numbers, and an artwork comparison are combined into one confidence. On our own 54-card test of simulated phone photos it named the right card about 65% of the time (the earlier approach managed about 24%), and it declines rather than inventing a name. Real-world results depend on light and focus.

Trading you can trust at a glance

Trades happen in a shared room on the host's phone over your local network, never a server of ours. Cards are marked physical or digital, a verification code appears on both phones, and nothing moves until both confirm.

Signed updates

Every release is signed with the same key, so a new version installs over the old one. We publish the SHA-256 of each APK so you can check you have the real file.

Open about limits

Collectify is young, independent software, so expect rough edges. It is not affiliated with TCGplayer, The Pokémon Company, Wizards of the Coast or any other publisher.

Check you're up to date, and that your file is genuine

Two ways: from inside the app, or by hand.

In the app

Tap the ℹ️ button, then Check for updates. It compares your installed version with the list above and, on Android, checks your installed APK's SHA-256 against the published one. If a newer version exists, Releases page opens it in your browser; install the new file over the app.

By hand

Compare your download with the hash in the table. On Windows PowerShell:

Get-FileHash .\Collectify-vX.Y.Z.apk -Algorithm SHA256

On macOS/Linux:

sha256sum Collectify-vX.Y.Z.apk

The data behind this page is public JSON: releases.json (every release) and latest.json (just the newest). A checksum only proves the file matches what we published; it can't tell you anything about a copy from somewhere else.

Questions

Will updating erase my binders?

No. Updates install over the existing app and keep your binders, wishlist, grades, trades and game progress, as long as the file is one of ours (same signing key). If you ever have an older build signed with a different key, you'd have to uninstall it first, which does remove local data.

Why isn't it on the Play Store?

It's distributed as a direct download for now. That's why Android asks you to allow installing it.

Where do the prices come from?

tcgcsv.com, which republishes TCGplayer's catalog and market prices. Treat them as estimates; condition adjustments are approximate.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No account exists, and your collection isn't uploaded. The app contacts tcgcsv.com if you sync prices, the TCGplayer image CDN to show card pictures, and this site when you tap "Check for updates". A live trade talks directly between the two phones on your network.
